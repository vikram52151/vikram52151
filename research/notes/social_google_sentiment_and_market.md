# Social / Review-Site Sentiment on Indian Restaurant POS & QR Ordering Vendors, Diner Attitudes, and Market Context

> **Method and access caveats (read first).** Research done 12 Oct 2026. In this environment, direct page fetches were blocked. Trustpilot, Capterra.in, SoftwareSuggest, Techjockey and Reddit all returned DNS or proxy 403 errors. The search tool also refuses reddit.com and several Indian news domains (livemint, thehindu, indianexpress, hindustantimes, TOI). Facebook groups, Instagram/YouTube comments, Google Maps reviews of vendor offices, MouthShut, consumercomplaints.in and akosha **could not be accessed**, and no search snippets from them turned up. Every review-site finding below comes from search-engine extracts or summaries of those pages, not from my own reading of each review. Quotes are short paraphrase-level extracts as the search tool returned them. Individual review dates were mostly not visible. Where a date is given it is the page or article date, unless the finding says otherwise. Treat all review-site material as **anecdotal** (small n, self-selected). Many "comparison" pages are written by competing vendors (DineOpen, OrderIt, Restrofi, Billoop and others) and are marked as such.

## Q1. Top 10 reasons small Indian F&B owners reject or abandon POS/QR software

### Takeaway
The complaints that come up most about Indian restaurant POS vendors are uneven or slow after-sales support (Petpooja, GoFrugal, Restroworks, DotPe) and money disputes: advance payments, refunds, hardware, and annual renewals. Next come peak-hour slowness or sync glitches, complex features, and weak offline mode. For QR/aggregator dine-in tools, the main owner-side objection is commission plus mandatory discounts (NRAI vs Zomato Pay/Swiggy Dineout). Hard evidence from small stalls specifically is very thin.

### Cited Findings
**Support quality (most frequent theme, both directions)**
- Petpooja: a SoftwareSuggest reviewer says "customer support is very poor," with "long response times and no proper resolution to issues." Another says advance payments were taken with no response, and staff were too busy onboarding new clients to fix old issues (individual review date not visible; page title "2026") — [SoftwareSuggest Petpooja reviews](https://www.softwaresuggest.com/petpooja/reviews)
- Petpooja: a Techjockey reviewer reports support calls not connecting and long hold times (page "2026") — [Techjockey Petpooja reviews](https://www.techjockey.com/reviews/petpooja-pos)
- Counter-evidence: a G2 reviewer says "support is awesome, you will get immediate support even at night." A Capterra reviewer says the team is reachable 24/7. G2 aggregate: 4.7/5 from 287 reviews, about half from small businesses (page 2026) — [G2 Petpooja reviews](https://www.g2.com/products/petpooja-poss/reviews); [Capterra Petpooja](https://www.capterra.com/p/172163/Petpooja-Restaurant-Management-Platform/reviews/)
- Competitor DineOpen (biased source) says multiple Google and G2 reviews mention slow Petpooja support, especially outside business hours (2026) — [DineOpen Petpooja review](https://www.dineopen.com/blog/petpooja-review-2026)
- GoFrugal: a 2024 Capterra reviewer: "Lot of cons, majorly the customer support team and their responsiveness when a major [issue]…". Another reviewer: "good response is seen only with partners", meaning support depends on the local dealer. Capterra lists 198 reviews — [Capterra GoFrugal reviews](https://www.capterra.com/p/71693/GoFrugal-POS-Software/reviews/); SoftwareSuggest summary notes "unsatisfactory 24/7 support" — [SoftwareSuggest GoFrugal reviews](https://www.softwaresuggest.com/gofrugal-retail/reviews)
- Restroworks/POSist: a Capterra reviewer says the "support helpline number is not reachable sometimes" (chat helped). Others report slower email replies and weaker weekend support — [Capterra Restroworks reviews](https://www.capterra.com/p/142877/Restroworks/reviews/)
- DotPe: Trustpilot shows 2.5/5 from only 4 reviews. Reviewers describe slow or missing responses after payment and frequent changes of account manager (very small n; dates not visible) — [Trustpilot dotpe.in](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/www.dotpe.in)

**Refunds, advance payments and hardware disputes**
- Petpooja (Trustpilot, Jan 2026): the reviewer returned a device to head office. Petpooja "confirmed my full refund in writing on 12 December 2025", promising payment by 26 Dec 2025, but "no UTR / transaction reference has been shared till date." **Total cost: hardware, add-ons, annual renewals**
- Third-party estimates (Petpooja does not publish prices): Core about ₹10,000/outlet/yr plus 18% GST, with higher tiers at about ₹20,000 and ₹40,000 — [Servyn guide](https://servyn.in/guides/petpooja-software-price-comparison/). Capterra lists an "Electron Poss Core" plan at ₹12,000 plus GST — [Capterra Petpooja](https://www.capterra.com/p/172163/Petpooja-Restaurant-Management-Platform/). - A buyer's guide warns of "one-time setup/installation fees (₹5,000–₹20,000), hardware purchase requirements, add-on fees, annual renewal lock-ins, and per-branch pricing" (vendor-authored, 2026) — [Billoop price guide](https://billoop.in/restaurant-billing-software-price)

Capterra pricing listings are inconsistent ($69/mo flat vs $200/user/yr) — [SoftwareSuggest Restroworks](https://www.softwaresuggest.com/restroworks); [Capterra Restroworks](https://www.capterra.com/p/142877/Restroworks/)
- Torqus is described in roundups as having "premium pricing for smaller restaurants", "not ideal for QSR" and a "smaller ecosystem" (vendor roundups, weak) — [Braincave](https://braincavesoft.com/post/top-10-restaurant-software-in-india); [Codingclave](https://codingclave.com/blog/best-restaurant-pos-software-india-2026)

**Product reliability and complexity**
- Petpooja: complaints of slowness and glitches during peak hours, inventory sync issues, and many confusing options without guidance (G2/Capterra summaries) — [G2 Petpooja vs Restroworks](https://www.g2.com/compare/petpooja-vs-restroworks-restaurant-pos); [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/172163/Petpooja-Restaurant-Management-Platform/reviews/)
- Restroworks: "if internet is down reporting features does not work". The backend is slow at week start and month close. Backdating is limited to 5 days — [Capterra Restroworks reviews](https://www.capterra.com/p/142877/Restroworks/reviews/); [SoftwareSuggest Restroworks](https://www.softwaresuggest.com/restroworks)
- GoFrugal: steep learning curve, dated interface, restaurant module "added later", limited QR ordering (claim from competitor OrderIt, biased) — [OrderIt GoFrugal alternatives](https://orderitnow.in/blog/top-7-best-gofrugal-restaurant-pos-alternatives-in-india-2026-features-pricing-comparison/)

**Aggregator dine-in tools (Zomato Pay / Swiggy Dineout/Diner): owner rejection**
- NRAI advisory (2022): restaurants in Zomato Pay/Swiggy Diner must give 15–40% discounts and pay 4–12% commission per transaction, against about 1–1.5% for payment gateways. The discounts apply even to diners who found the restaurant on their own — [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/zomato-pay-swiggy-diner-may-adversely-impact-restaurant-industry-in-the-long-run-nrai/); [Outlook Startup](https://startup.outlookindia.com/analysis/zomato-pay-swiggy-diner-discount-progs-against-interest-of-restaurant-owners-nrai-news-6282)
- In the 2022 Dineout dispute, NRAI asked members to log out. It claimed more than 400 brands and about 900 outlets sent delisting notices — [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/swiggys-dineout-runs-into-trouble-nrai-asks-restaurants-to-log-out-of-the-app/); [The Federal](https://thefederal.com/explainers-2/explained-why-nearly-1000-restaurants-are-quitting-swiggy-dineout)
- Jan 2025: NRAI complaint over aggregators' private-label brands and separate quick-delivery apps — [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/business/companies/nrai-to-file-complaint-against-zomato-swiggy-over-private-labelling-food-delivery-via-separate-apps-3348390). The CCI probe reportedly found anti-competitive conduct (from unnamed sources; final order status unknown) — [Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/business/competition-commission-probe-finds-zomato-swiggy-violating-competition-norms/)

**Vendor failure risk**
- Thrive (a direct-ordering, roughly 3% commission alternative to aggregators, backed by Jubilant and Coca-Cola, with 14,000+ restaurants in 80 cities) has shut down. The CEO cited a "lack of resources" in a market "dominated by few well-funded giants." Exact closure date not confirmed in snippets — [afaqs](https://www.afaqs.com/news/brands/thrive-calls-it-a-day-after-5-years-dhruv-dewan-moves-to-tata-digital-9335904); [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/coca-cola-backed-foodtech-startup-thrive-shuts-operations/)

### Inferences
- A synthesised top-10 list of rejection and abandonment reasons, ranked roughly by how often they appeared. This is my synthesis, not a survey:
  1. Slow or unreachable support after the sale; teams prioritise onboarding new customers.
  2. Refund and advance-payment disputes.
  3. High year-1 cost once hardware, add-ons and GST are counted.
  4. Annual prepaid contracts and renewal lock-ins.
  5. Hardware pushed by commissioned salespeople.
  6. Peak-hour slowness and sync glitches.
  7. Complexity or a learning curve too steep for small staff.
  8. Weak offline behaviour when the internet drops.
  9. Aggregator dine-in tools take commission and force discounts.
  10. Vendor-survival risk (Thrive shutdown) and dealer-dependent support quality (GoFrugal).
- For tea stalls specifically, the strongest implied barrier is that UPI QR plus a notebook is free (see Q4). Any recurring fee competes with zero.

### Gaps
- No Reddit, Facebook-group, Instagram/YouTube-comment, Google Maps or MouthShut evidence could be retrieved (access blocked or no snippets).
- I found no survey quantifying why small Indian outlets churn from POS vendors.
- No auto-renewal-specific complaint (an unconsented card or UPI-mandate charge) was found. The "renewal lock-in" evidence comes from vendor-authored guides only.
- No complaints were found for Rista, Torqus, or Zomato/Swiggy QR dine-in tools from individual owners after 2022.

## Q2. Top reasons owners pick (and stay with) a vendor

### Takeaway
Positive reviews stress support responsiveness (when it works), ease of use, and billing/GST features. Long tenure with Petpooja is linked to early free or cheap pricing. Aggregator integration and commission savings drive QR/direct-ordering adoption (DotPe). For GoFrugal, a good local partner is what keeps owners. All of this is review-site anecdote; no independent selection survey was found.

### Cited Findings
- Petpooja: a GetApp reviewer says all their restaurants still run on it after using it "since the free-subscription days" (date not visible) — [GetApp Petpooja](https://www.getapp.com/all-software/a/petpooja/reviews/)
- Petpooja: G2 reviewers praise the "user-friendly interface and excellent customer support." G2 summaries say Petpooja edges out Restroworks on "more intuitive setup," while Restroworks scores higher on inventory — [G2 Petpooja vs Restroworks](https://www.g2.com/compare/petpooja-vs-restroworks-restaurant-pos)
- Restroworks: a G2 reviewer says "the pricing is also good" and that they got extra discounts. The Restroworks Academy training is praised for onboarding staff — [G2 Restroworks](https://www.g2.com/products/restroworks-restaurant-pos/reviews)
- GoFrugal: a tier-3-city restaurant owner praises the local partner's training and support (Capterra) — [Capterra GoFrugal](https://www.capterra.com/p/71693/GoFrugal-POS-Software/reviews/)
- DotPe: a Slashdot reviewer likes that its QR plus payment gateway "work well alongside Swiggy and Zomato integration," but notes "no marketing done from this company" — [Slashdot DotPe](https://slashdot.org/software/p/DotPe/). DotPe's own testimonial (Wat-a-Burger co-founder) claims its QR solutions "reduced their 3rd party commission by 98%" (vendor marketing) — [DotPe](https://dotpe.in/)
- Rista claims "100k+ merchants" (company claim) and is listed from about ₹1,500/mo for small cafes and QSRs (roundup) — [Rista](https://ristaapps.com/); [Codingclave](https://codingclave.com/blog/best-restaurant-pos-software-india-2026)
- Petpooja is reported to dominate Tier-2/3 India (competitor claim) — [Restrofi](https://restrofi.com/blog/restaurant-pos-alternatives-india)

### Inferences
- Pick drivers, ordered by evidence strength:
  1. Responsive local or human support.
  2. Ease of setup and use.
  3. Price, including free or legacy plans.
  4. Aggregator integration (Swiggy/Zomato) and lower commission through direct QR ordering.
  5. Brand ubiquity in Tier-2/3 cities.
  6. Local dealer presence.
- Language support and hardware bundling as positive pick factors were **not** evidenced in the retrieved reviews. Hardware bundling showed up more as a complaint than a draw.

### Gaps
- No evidence on regional-language UI as a decision factor.
- No independent survey of vendor-selection criteria among Indian small F&B outlets.

## Q3. Diner attitudes to QR menus and ordering in India

### Takeaway
There is **no credible India-specific diner survey** on QR menus in what I could retrieve. The best quantitative data is US-based: about 81–88% prefer paper menus. India-specific evidence is anecdotal, apart from a high-profile 2026 privacy incident in Pune involving phone numbers collected through a QR menu, which shows the risk of mandatory mobile-number capture. UPI habits are very strong (see Q5), so QR *payment* is normal, while QR *menus and ordering* remain contested.

### Cited Findings
- US, Toast survey of 850 adults: 81% prefer a physical menu, only 1% prefer QR codes; about 90% of those 55+ prefer physical. Fieldwork date disputed (Sept 2024 vs 2025) — [Toast](https://pos.toasttab.com/blog/on-the-line/qr-code-menu-insights); [QRStuff](https://www.qrstuff.com/feeds/blog/qr-menu-vs-paper-menu-better); [Duck-hub](https://www.duck-hub.com/blog/qr-code-menu-statistics)
- US, Technomic, May 2022 (n=1,000): 88% prefer paper menus (Gen Z 86%, boomers 95%), and 57% say QR menus feel like "a chore" — [Restaurant Business](https://www.restaurantbusinessonline.com/technology/customers-really-dont-qr-code-menus)
- India, Pune, May 2026 (viral Instagram story): a woman alleged a restaurant employee used her phone number, captured via the QR-menu system, to send late-night texts. The restaurant said the employee was fired — [India TV, 14 May 2026](https://www.indiatvnews.com/trending/news/pune-woman-qr-code-menu-restaurant-employee-late-night-messages-viral-video-harassment-2026-05-14-1041098); [Asianet Newsable](https://newsable.asianetnews.com/india/pune-shocker-qr-code-to-personal-texts-woman-shares-disturbing-restaurant-incident-watch-articleshow-nl944gu)
- India trade press: QR menus have "become the new normal" in Delhi and Mumbai (opinion, not a survey). For older or rural diners the shift "can be intimidating," and smartphone and internet connectivity "is still inconsistent in certain parts of the country" — [Restaurant India](https://www.restaurantindia.in/article/how-qr-codes-have-transformed-the-guest-experience-in-the-indian-restaurant-industry.13019); [Restaurant India](https://www.restaurantindia.in/article/the-digital-transformation-of-menus-why-qr-codes-and-apps-are-here-to-stay.11653)
- Unverified vendor claims (weak): "74% of diners under 45 comfortable with QR menus" (no source) — [DineCard](https://www.dinecard.in/blog/qr-code-menu-vs-paper-menu-comparison). "68% of Indian diners prefer contactless ordering" (attributed to a "2025 NASSCOM report" I could not verify). Case studies with "15% requesting paper" — [DineOpen](https://www.dineopen.com/blog/qr-code-menus-future-trend-2026.html)
- Connectivity complaints such as no cell reception in the building appear in non-India Reddit threads (as summarised by search; not India-specific) — [Menyo blog](https://www.menyo.pro/blog/qr-menu-backlash-why-customers-hate-digital-ordering)

### Inferences
- In India, giving out a mobile number is the most salient diner-side QR friction (privacy, spam, the Pune incident). A QR flow that needs no OTP or phone number would remove it.
- Vendors themselves recommend offering both QR and paper menus, which suggests a sizeable minority still wants paper (weak).
- Phone battery and data are plausible frictions but **no India-specific data was found**.

### Gaps
- No India-specific diner survey (e.g., LocalCircles) on QR menus was found.
- No Reddit, Instagram or YouTube diner comments could be retrieved.
- Battery and data friction is not quantified for India.

## Q4. Price point and pricing model that resonate with tea stalls and small cafes

### Takeaway
The market anchor for tiny outlets is **₹0 to about ₹250/month** (free tiers, ₹150–199/mo apps, ₹799–2,999/yr plans). Mid-market cloud POS sits at about ₹750–1,500/mo. Petpooja-class incumbents run about ₹10,000–20,000/yr plus hardware. Owners are strongly fee-averse even at fractions of a percent: only 17% of 32,796 merchants would accept a UPI MDR of 0.3% or more, and 41% would accept none. A free tier, or one with no fixed fee and a small usage charge, fits the evidence best. Large annual prepay plus hardware fits worst.

### Cited Findings
- Vendor guide price bands: "single outlet (tea stall / dhaba) ₹0–₹500/mo for basic billing, GST invoicing, receipt printer, with free plans available" — [OrderIt under-₹5,000 guide](https://orderitnow.in/blog/best-free-affordable-restaurant-billing-software-in-india-2026-under-%E2%82%B95000-year/) (vendor, 2026)
- Listed price points (vendor sites, 2026):
  - myBillBook from ₹199/mo — [myBillBook](https://mybillbook.in/s/restaurant-billing-software/)
  - OrderIt from ₹150/mo — [OrderIt cafe](https://orderitnow.in/blog/cafe-bakery-billing-software-india-2026-best-pos-for-small-shops/)
  - BillXact ₹799/yr — [BillXact](https://billxact.com/billing-software-for-restaurants/)
  - Setuverse ₹2,999/yr plus GST (about ₹250/mo) — [Setuverse](https://www.setuverse.com/cafe-billing-software)
  - SynexPOS ₹749/outlet/mo flat — [Synexlabs](https://synexlabs.in/blog/top-10-restaurant-billing-software-india-2026)
  - Deelo tea-shop plan: no subscription, per-order commission — [Deelo](https://shops.deelo.in/for/tea-shops)
  - Rista from about ₹1,500/mo — [Codingclave](https://codingclave.com/blog/best-restaurant-pos-software-india-2026)
- Petpooja: about ₹10,000/outlet/yr (some sources ₹12,000) plus GST, renewal about ₹7,500/yr. Hardware is extra (see Q1) — [Servyn](https://servyn.in/guides/petpooja-software-price-comparison/); [SaaSworthy](https://www.saasworthy.com/product/petpooja/pricing)
- Fee aversion (LocalCircles merchant survey, Sept 2026, n=32,796 across 242 districts):
  - 17% willing to bear an MDR of 0.3% or more on UPI payments above ₹2,000 (some coverage says 0.4%).
  - 41% unwilling to bear any MDR, and 9% don't accept UPI at all.
  - Willingness drops from 50% at 0.04% to 35% at 0.1% and 25% at 0.25%.
  - Source: [LocalCircles press](https://www.localcircles.com/a/press/page/upi-merchant-survey); [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/news/only-17-merchants-willing-to-bear-0-4-mdr-on-upi-payments-survey-126091501429_1.html)
- The Retailers Association of India warned the UPI merchant fee could push small retailers back toward cash (Sept 2026) — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/news/rai-warns-upi-merchant-fee-could-push-small-retailers-back-towards-cash-126091601362_1.html)
- Commission model precedent: Thrive charged about 3% per order and still failed commercially — [afaqs](https://www.afaqs.com/news/brands/thrive-calls-it-a-day-after-5-years-dhruv-dewan-moves-to-tata-digital-9335904). NRAI calls 4–12% dine-in commissions unacceptable against 1–1.5% payment-gateway rates (2022) — [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/zomato-pay-swiggy-diner-may-adversely-impact-restaurant-industry-in-the-long-run-nrai/)
- Petpooja retention anecdote: long-term customers who started on free subscriptions — [GetApp](https://www.getapp.com/all-software/a/petpooja/reviews/)

### Inferences
- **Tea stalls and kiosks:** free core, with an optional upgrade at about ₹99–199/mo, works on the owner's own phone (no hardware) and stays cancellable monthly. Percentage commissions on dine-in orders are likely to be resisted. Even 0.1–0.3% fees meet majority resistance among merchants (LocalCircles), though that survey concerns payment MDR, not software.
- **Small cafes:** about ₹150–750/mo, or a ₹1,500–3,000/yr prepaid option with a discount. The ₹10k+/yr incumbent tier is the "upgrade" segment.
- One-time licences with no renewal are attractive in principle but **no direct evidence** was found.

### Gaps
- No owner-voiced (forum or social) statements of an acceptable ₹/month were retrievable. All price points are vendor list prices, not willingness-to-pay data.
- No survey splits monthly vs annual vs commission preference among Indian F&B owners.

## Q5. Market context: outlets, POS penetration, UPI at food stalls, QR ordering adoption (2023–2026)

### Takeaway
The NRAI IFSR 2024 values Indian food services at ₹5.69 lakh crore (FY24), 56.2% unorganised, growing at 8.1% CAGR to ₹7.77 lakh crore by FY28. City outlet counts are large (Mumbai 141,456 organised plus unorganised). UPI is near-universal among small merchants (94% adoption in a Feb 2026 government study; 658M QR codes deployed). Small merchants up to ₹1 lakh/month stay exempt from the new 0.4% MDR from 15 Oct 2026. No credible figure exists for POS penetration among small outlets. The only sourced QR-ordering adoption figure is about one-third of restaurants during COVID, from a vendor (POSist) report.

### Cited Findings
- NRAI IFSR 2024 (released 9 Jul 2024):
  - Market ₹5,69,487 cr FY24, reaching ₹7,76,511 cr by FY28 (8.1% CAGR); organised segment growing 13.2%.
  - Unorganised share falls from 56.2% to 47.1% by 2028.
  - 85.5 lakh employed (2024); about 1.9% of GDP.
  - Sources: [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/industry/news/indian-food-services-sector-to-grow-by-8-1-from-2024-to-2028-report-124070900997_1.html); [Restaurant India](https://www.restaurantindia.in/article/india-to-be-the-3rd-largest-food-service-market-by-2028-overtaking-japan-nrai-ifsr-2024); [HospitalityBiz](https://hospitalitybizindia.com/news-track/food-services-sector-to-grow-by-8-1-cagr-during-2024-2028-says-nrais-india-food-services-report-2024/)
- City outlet counts from IFSR 2024 (organised plus unorganised): Mumbai 141,456; Hyderabad 74,807; Goa 15,147 — [Audience Reports / Hospitality Lexis](https://hospitalitylexis.media/national-restaurant-association-of-india-nrai-released-its-india-food-services-report-2024-nrai-ifsr-hyderabad-food-services-industry-estimated-to-be-valued-at-%E2%82%B9-10161-crores-in-the-orga/); [Goa TV24](https://goatv24.in/archives/4194)
- UPI and small merchants:
  - DFS/Ministry of Finance study (Feb 2026; 10,378 respondents, 15 states): 94% of small merchants surveyed have adopted UPI, and 57% report higher sales after going digital (not attributable to UPI alone).
  - UPI QR deployments grew from 93M to about 658M.
  - Sources: [Business Standard, Aug 2026](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/news/small-merchants-built-upi-s-reach-here-s-what-government-data-shows-126081700057_1.html)
- UPI MDR change: a 0.4% MDR on P2M UPI payments above ₹2,000 applies from 15 Oct 2026. Small merchants (P2PM, up to ₹1 lakh/month via UPI QR, including street vendors) are exempt. ### Inferences
- Payment digitisation at stalls is essentially solved by free UPI QR. The gap is in ordering, menus and billing, where most unorganised outlets (more than 56% of value, and likely a larger share of outlet count) remain undigitised.
- The 15 Oct 2026 MDR exemption keeps UPI free for stalls, so a free UPI-linked ordering layer has no payment-cost headwind.
